## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes **failed ride requests from the Gett (GetTaxi) platform** to understand why customers fail to complete their rides.

The analysis was performed using **Microsoft Fabric and PySpark**, following a **Bronze → Silver → Gold** data architecture.

The project focuses on:

- Identifying major ride failure reasons
- Analyzing driver assignment vs. unassigned requests
- Understanding customer cancellation behavior
- Measuring time to cancellation
- Analyzing ETA impact
- Identifying high-risk time periods
- Finding driver supply and matching bottlenecks
- Developing data-driven business recommendations

The project goes beyond descriptive analysis by converting analytical findings into **business recommendations and measurable experiments**.

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

Ride failures directly impact:

- Customer satisfaction
- Revenue
- Driver utilization
- Marketplace efficiency
- Customer retention

### Key Business Question

> **Why are customers failing to complete rides, and what can Gett do to reduce these failures?**

The analysis specifically separates failures into:

1. Customer cancellation before driver assignment
2. Customer cancellation after driver assignment
3. System rejection before driver assignment
4. System rejection after driver assignment

This helps identify whether the problem is primarily related to **driver availability and matching** or the **post-assignment customer experience**.

# 📊 Executive KPI Summary

| KPI | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total Failed Orders | **31,268** |
| Customer Cancellation Rate | **69.70%** |
| Average Platform ETA | **7.92 min** |
| Average Time to Cancellation | **2.74 min** |
| Failures Without Driver Assignment | **22,904** |
| Share of Failures Without Assignment | **73.24%** |

### 🚨 Key Business Insight

> **73.24% of failed orders occurred before a driver was assigned.**

This indicates that **driver availability, initial matching speed, and the early customer experience** are the biggest areas of opportunity.

# 🔎 Key Findings

## 1. Most Failures Happen Before Driver Assignment

| Failure Type | Orders | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cancelled + Unassigned | 13,435 | 42.96% |
| Cancelled + Assigned | 8,360 | 26.74% |
| System Reject + Unassigned | 9,469 | 30.28% |
| System Reject + Assigned | 4 | 0.02% |

### Insight

The largest individual failure category is:

> **Customer cancellation while no driver has been assigned — 13,435 orders.**

When combined with unassigned system rejections:

```text
13,435 + 9,469 = 22,904
```

Therefore:

> **73.24% of all failed orders happened before driver assignment.**

### Business Implication

The company should prioritize:

- Faster driver matching
- Better driver availability
- Smarter dispatch rules
- Demand/supply balancing
- Improved communication while searching for a driver

---

# ⏱️ 2. The First Two Minutes Are a Critical Window

Customers without a driver assigned cancelled after approximately:

> **2.01 minutes on average**

Customers with a driver assigned generally waited longer before cancelling:

> **3.20–5.07 minutes**

### What does this mean?

Once a customer knows that a driver has been found, they appear more willing to wait.

However, when the platform continues searching without assigning a driver, the customer reaches a cancellation point much faster.

---

## 💡 Recommended 90-Second Intervention

The analysis recommends **testing an intervention around 90 seconds** for rides where no driver has been assigned.

This does **not** mean giving every customer a discount.

Instead, the platform could use a staged approach:

| Time | Possible Action |
|---|---|
| 0–30 sec | Normal driver matching |
| 30–60 sec | Show progress/status if matching is taking longer |
| 60–90 sec | Prioritize nearby driver search |
| ~90 sec | Send proactive customer update |
| ~90 sec | Test targeted incentive for high-risk customers |
| ~2 min+ | Escalated matching/retention strategy |

### Example Customer Journey

**10:00:00 — Ride requested**

> "Finding a driver near you..."

**10:01:00 — No driver assigned**

> "We're still searching for the closest available driver."

**10:01:30 — 90-second intervention**

> "We're experiencing high demand and are still looking for the closest available driver."

For selected high-risk customers, the platform could test:

> "Sorry for the wait. Continue with your ride and receive ₹20 off."

**10:02:00 — High-risk cancellation window**

The customer reaches approximately the observed average cancellation point for unassigned rides.

### ⚠️ Important

The **90-second threshold is a data-informed experiment**, not a proven universal customer limit.

It should be validated using **A/B testing** before being implemented at scale.

Potential issues:

# 🚗 4. Driver Assignment Has a Major Impact

| Driver Status | Average Cancellation Timing |
|---|---:|
| No Driver Assigned | **2.01 min** |
| Driver Assigned | **3.20–5.07 min** |

### Business Interpretation

Getting a driver assigned is not simply an operational step.

It changes the customer experience from:

> ❌ "Nobody is accepting my ride."

to:

> ✅ "My driver is coming."

Therefore, improving **time-to-assignment** may be more valuable than focusing only on reducing ETA after assignment.

# 📍 5. ETA Analysis

Assigned cancellations were analyzed across ETA ranges.

| ETA Bucket | Cancelled Orders | Median ETA |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5–8 min | **2,531** | 6 min |
| 1–4 min | **2,446** | 3 min |
| 9–12 min | 1,758 | 10 min |
| 13–16 min | 1,279 | 14 min |
| 14–20 min | 320 | 18 min |

### Key Insight

Cancellation is **not caused only by long ETA**.

A large number of cancellations occur even when ETA is relatively short:

> **1–8 minutes**

This suggests other factors may influence cancellation:

- ETA changes after assignment
- Driver movement/stalling
- Pickup location difficulty
- Customer changing plans
- Driver behavior
- ETA accuracy
- Communication quality

### Recommended Next Analysis

Investigate:

```text
Initial ETA
     ↓
Updated ETA
     ↓
Driver Movement
     ↓
Actual Pickup Time
     ↓
Cancellation
```

This can help determine whether **ETA instability** is contributing to customer cancellations.

# 💼 Strategic Recommendations

## 1. Protect the First 90 Seconds

Create an operational trigger for unassigned rides around the 90-second mark.

### Actions

- Improve driver search priority
- Show meaningful progress updates
- Display realistic search expectations
- Test targeted incentives for high-risk customers

### KPIs

- Assignment rate within 90 seconds
- Cancellation rate after 90 seconds
- Average time to assignment
- Completed rides
- Cost per saved ride

---

## 2. Improve Driver Supply During High-Risk Periods

Use demand forecasting to identify periods with insufficient driver supply.

Focus initially on:

- Evening Peak
- Night Shift
- High-demand locations

### Potential Strategy

Use **targeted driver incentives** instead of blanket incentives.

Example:

> "High demand expected between 8 PM–11 PM in Zone A."

Drivers could receive additional incentives for staying online or accepting rides during that period.

### KPIs

- Driver availability
- Unassigned rate
- System rejection rate
- Completed rides per active driver

---

## 3. Reposition Drivers Before Morning Peak

Morning Peak shows a relatively high ETA:

> **10.3 minutes**

Instead of waiting for demand to appear, drivers could be positioned near high-demand pickup zones **before the peak begins**.

### Expected Impact

- Lower ETA
- Faster assignment
- Higher ride completion
- Better driver utilization

---

## 4. Use Different Strategies for Different Shifts

A single global cancellation strategy may not work.

| Period | Recommended Focus |
|---|---|
| Mid-Day | Customer retention + matching experience |
| Evening Peak | Driver supply |
| Morning Peak | Driver positioning |
| Night Shift | Driver availability + system rejection |
| Mid-Night | Supply monitoring |

---

## 5. Investigate Post-Assignment Cancellations

Because many assigned cancellations occur at short/medium ETA ranges, investigate:

- Driver movement
- ETA changes
- Pickup distance
- Pickup location
- Driver acceptance behavior
- Customer cancellation timing

The objective is to determine whether the problem lies with:

```text
Customer
   ↓
Driver
   ↓
Dispatch
   ↓
ETA
   ↓
Pickup Location
```

# 🧪 Recommended A/B Tests

Before implementing recommendations at scale, conduct controlled experiments.

| Experiment | Control | Test | Primary KPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90-sec status update | Existing experience | Proactive message | Cancellation rate |
| Targeted discount | No discount | High-risk customer offer | Incremental completed rides |
| Driver repositioning | Current positioning | Pre-positioned drivers | Median ETA |
| Night incentive | Existing incentive | Targeted night incentive | Unassigned rate |
| ETA communication | Existing display | Improved ETA messaging | Assigned cancellation rate |

### ⚠️ Business Success Metric

Experiments should not be judged only by:

> "Did cancellations decrease?"

The real question is:

> **"Did additional completed rides generate enough value to justify the cost of the intervention?"**

For example, a ₹20 discount that saves a ride worth only ₹10 in contribution margin would **not** be a successful business strategy.

# ⚠️ Analytical Limitations

This analysis is **descriptive**, meaning it identifies patterns and opportunities but does not prove causality.

Important considerations:

- The 2.01-minute cancellation timing is an observed average, not a universal customer limit.
- A high ETA does not necessarily cause cancellation.
- Discounts may reduce cancellations but can also reduce profitability.
- The 90-second intervention should be validated through experimentation.
- ETA bucket definitions should be standardized before production reporting.

---

# 🎯 Final Business Conclusion

The analysis reveals a clear operational opportunity:

> **Gett is losing a large proportion of potential rides before a driver is assigned.**

With **73.24% of failures occurring without driver assignment**, the primary opportunity is to improve the early ride-matching experience.

### Recommended Strategy

```text
Faster Matching
       ↓
Better Driver Availability
       ↓
Clear Customer Communication
       ↓
90-Second Intervention
       ↓
Targeted Retention
       ↓
Higher Ride Completion
```

The most important next step is **not simply launching discounts**.

Instead, Gett should run controlled experiments to determine which intervention produces the highest number of **incremental completed rides at an acceptable cost**.
